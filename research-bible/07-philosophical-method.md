# Philosophical Method

## Operations

1. **Exposition:** establish what the text says.
2. **Reconstruction:** express premises, assumptions, mechanisms, and conclusions.
3. **Comparison:** compare equivalent questions without erasing conceptual or historical differences.
4. **Analysis:** test meanings, distinctions, presuppositions, and explanatory roles.
5. **Evaluation:** assess validity, coherence, evidence, scope, implications, and objections.
6. **Historical contextualization:** use context to interpret and test claims, not replace philosophical argument.

## Argument worksheet

For each major claim record: premises → conceptual assumptions → historical/empirical evidence → causal mechanism → conclusion → philosophical implication → strongest objection → Fukuyamian response → residual problem.

## Evaluation standard

Assess explanatory power, clarity, historical adequacy, comparative breadth, contingency, causation, anthropology, biology/politics, sequencing, ideas/material conditions, universality, Eurocentrism where sourced, determinism, path dependence, normative assumptions, strong-state/liberal-institution relations, contemporary applicability, explanation/prescription, and limits of large-scale comparison.
