# Research Question

## Central question

How does Francis Fukuyama explain the historical emergence, differentiation, interaction, and decay of the institutions necessary for political order—especially the state, rule of law, and political accountability—and how adequate is this framework philosophically?

## Subsidiary questions

1. What philosophical anthropology supports Fukuyama's account of natural sociability, kinship, recognition, and institutional development?
2. How does his evolutionary account compare with the state-of-nature constructions of Hobbes, Locke, and Rousseau?
3. What makes an institution a state, rule of law, or accountability mechanism in his usage?
4. Which mechanisms explain movement beyond kinship and toward impersonal administration?
5. How do China, India, Islamic political orders, and Western Europe test or qualify the theory?
6. Does the institutional triad describe, explain, or prescribe political order—or combine these roles?
7. How well does the account handle contingency, sequencing, ideas, material forces, universality, and decay?

## Working thesis status

No final thesis is fixed. The abstract contains a provisional favorable assessment plus questions about universality, diversity, and contingency. Treat this as student-authored orientation, not a conclusion already proved.
