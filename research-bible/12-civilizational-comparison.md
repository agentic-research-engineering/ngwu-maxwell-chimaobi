# Civilizational Comparison Matrix

All entries are provisional reconstructions of Fukuyama and require independent specialist verification. Avoid treating internally diverse traditions as unitary.

| Dimension | China | India | Islamic political orders | Western Europe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kinship/route beyond tribe | Warfare and anti-kin reforms weaken lineage power | Kin/caste and religious social structure persist alongside states | Religious community and military slavery can bypass lineage | Church marriage/property rules weaken extended kin structures |
| Religion/ideas | Confucian–Legalist tension; ruler not institutionally subject to transcendent law | Brahmanical religious authority constrains rulers | Religious law has autonomy; mosque/state relation differs by time/place | Organized church gains corporate autonomy and legal authority |
| State formation | Early centralized, bureaucratic, coercively capable state | Recurrent states but weaker durable centralization in Fukuyama's account | Caliphal/sultanate/Ottoman forms; slave military-administrative systems | Late and uneven consolidation under feudal/social constraints |
| Bureaucracy/impersonality | Selection/administration develops early, periodically repatrimonialized | Degree and durability disputed | Mamluk/Janissary recruitment initially limits hereditary capture | Church and royal administrations supply corporate/legal models |
| Law versus ruler | Rule by law more than autonomous rule of law | Religious law/society can stand above political rulers | Sharia/juristic authority constrains rulers, with variation | Papal revolution and revived Roman/canon law support autonomous legality |
| Accountability | Weak formal social accountability to centralized ruler | Social/religious constraint, not necessarily representative accountability | Consultative/corporate constraints vary; needs precise treatment | Estates, law, taxation bargains, parliaments develop unevenly |
| Elite/patrimonial pressure | Families/elites recapture office and land | Caste/local structures limit and organize power | Hereditary normalization of slave elites drives repatrimonialization | Aristocratic privilege and rent-seeking both constrain and capture states |
| Sequencing | State first | Law/social constraint before strong state | Law and state follow distinct institutional tracks | Law before strong state, then accountability/state bargains |
| Philosophical significance | Capacity without full legal/accountable restraint | Social norm and law may restrain but fragment authority | Impersonal devices can be deliberately constructed yet decay | Balanced institutions emerge contingently, not from one blueprint |
| Primary locations | *Origins* chs. 6–9, 20–21 | chs. 10–12, 19 | chs. 13–15, 19 | chs. 16–18, 22–28 |
| Major critical need | “First modern state”; violence; bureaucracy accuracy | “Detour,” caste/religion generalization | Diversity, slavery, decline narrative | Eurocentrism, church causality, exceptionalism |

For each case add warfare, elite structure, decay, exact mechanism, quotations/pages, historical variation, and scholarly criticism before drafting.
