# Fukuyama Argument Map

## Developmental reconstruction

`[INTERPRETATION][PARTIALLY VERIFIED]`

Evolved human nature → natural sociability (kin selection, reciprocity, norm-following, recognition) → family/band/tribal organization → scale and collective-action problems → warfare, religion, leadership and other transition mechanisms → centralized state → struggle for impersonal administration → law autonomous from rulers → social constraints/accountability → possible balanced order.

This is not a single necessary sequence. Fukuyama's comparative cases exhibit different ordering, mechanisms, and incomplete combinations.

## Institutional triad

- **State:** concentrates and executes legitimate authority; supplies capacity.
- **Rule of law:** binds rulers through law not reducible to current political command.
- **Political accountability:** makes rulers answerable to organized social actors.
- **Balanced order:** requires differentiation plus a workable relation among all three; democracy is one modern accountability form, not a synonym for the triad.

## Decay reconstruction

Institutional rigidity + environmental change + elite capture + persistent kin/familial interests → erosion of impersonality → patrimonial or clientelistic appropriation → failure of adaptation/effective execution → political decay. Violence or crisis may disrupt equilibrium but does not guarantee improvement.
