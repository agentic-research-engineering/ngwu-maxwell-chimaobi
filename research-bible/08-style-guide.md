# Style Guide

Write formal undergraduate Philosophy prose that is precise, clear, argumentative, evidence-based, historically responsible, and readable.

- State the question and claim early; paragraphs should advance reasons, evidence, analysis, or objections.
- Attribute positions precisely: “Fukuyama argues…” only with primary support; “this study interprets…” for reconstruction.
- Preserve qualifiers and disputes. Define technical terms at first substantive use.
- Prefer connected argument to lists in final chapter prose.
- Avoid inflated diction, generic transitions, repeated conclusions, unsupported generalizations, fake quotations/citations, rhetorical padding, and excessive subheadings.
- Use one consistent citation style when the department's required style is confirmed. Until then, preserve full bibliographic data and page locations without silently converting styles.
- Do not present AI assistance as scholarship or obscure researcher authorship.
