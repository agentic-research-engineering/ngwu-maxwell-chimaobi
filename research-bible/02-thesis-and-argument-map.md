# Thesis and Argument Map

## Governing Thesis — Phase 3 Locked

`[RESEARCHER-THESIS]` This study argues that Fukuyama's triad of state capacity, rule of law, and political accountability is most defensible as a comparative diagnostic framework for understanding political order rather than as a universal evolutionary sequence. Its philosophical value lies in distinguishing the permanent tensions among political capacity, legal constraint, and responsiveness; its principal limitation arises where broad functional categories—such as the modern state, accountability, weakened kinship, and institutional convergence—compress historically plural mechanisms into a common developmental framework.

This thesis may be qualified only through a documented evidence conflict. It must not be silently replaced during drafting.

## Project-level argument

1. Establish Fukuyama's concepts and philosophical anthropology.
2. Reconstruct the triad and the mechanisms joining human sociability, kinship, state formation, impersonality, law, and accountability.
3. test the reconstruction against his civilizational comparisons.
4. distinguish descriptive genealogy, causal explanation, and normative evaluation.
5. evaluate explanatory power, clarity, historical adequacy, contingency, alternative paths, and contemporary relevance.
6. reach a qualified conclusion proportionate to verified evidence.

## Audit questions

- Does each conclusion follow from stated premises?
- Are historical examples evidence, illustration, or analogy?
- Does a change in concept occur between civilizations or volumes?
- Are rival explanations stated in their strongest form?
- Is the project's criticism independent of mere disagreement with outcomes?
