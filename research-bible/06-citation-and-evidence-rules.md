# Citation and Evidence Rules

## Evidence labels

- `[FUKUYAMA]`: explicit statement or clearly developed argument by Fukuyama.
- `[PRIMARY-OTHER]`: explicit statement by another primary author.
- `[SCHOLAR]`: attributable secondary scholarly claim.
- `[INTERPRETATION]`: this project's textual/conceptual reading.
- `[INFERENCE]`: supported but unstated conclusion.
- `[CONTESTED]`: identifiable scholarly disagreement.
- `[UNVERIFIED]`: insufficient verification.
- `[RESEARCHER-THESIS]`: argument advanced by this project.

Never relabel interpretation, inference, or researcher thesis as Fukuyama's claim without primary textual proof.

## Verification protocol

For direct quotations verify exact wording, source identity, edition, printed page/location, and sufficient context. For paraphrases record the passage that entails the paraphrase. Important assertions require page-specific evidence where stable pagination exists. Never invent pages, chapters, publication data, DOI, URL, author, title, historical fact, or statistic. Mark uncertainty `[UNVERIFIED]` or `[CITATION NEEDED]`.

## Source-to-claim test

Before citation, ask: (1) Does the source assert this? (2) Is it authoritative for this kind of claim? (3) Does the cited location support the whole sentence? (4) Have qualifiers survived paraphrase? (5) Is contrary evidence recorded?

## Locations

Record both printed page and PDF page when they differ: `print p. X; PDF p. Y`. Until edition pagination is verified, cite chapter/section and mark the page `[VERIFY]`.

## Web research

Use when the repository lacks necessary evidence. Prefer primary publishers/texts, journal databases, university presses/repositories, official institutions, and scholarly encyclopedias. Search snippets and AI summaries are discovery aids only. Add every adopted source to file 16 with access details and verification status.
