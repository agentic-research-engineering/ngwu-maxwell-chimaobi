## Strengths Established

1. The triad differentiates effective public power, legal constraint, and political responsiveness.
2. It diagnoses institutional combinations that democracy, legality, or capacity alone cannot explain.
3. It gives political institutions historical depth rather than assuming them as freely transferable designs.
4. It embeds political order in kinship, norms, status, ideas, organizations, and material pressures without reducing outcomes to biology.
5. It accommodates plural causation and different institutional sequences.
6. It connects institutional construction to rigidity, capture, and decay.
7. It treats development as reversible and political balance as managed tension rather than final equilibrium.

## Major Criticisms

1. Broad functional categories can lose analytical discrimination.
2. Fukuyama's accountability category risks blurring responsiveness, answerability, sanctions, representation, and electoral control.
3. Calling Qin/Han China “modern” is coherent only under a restricted administrative stipulation and risks importing wider meanings.
4. The Indian comparison supports a pattern of relatively autonomous social authority more strongly than a claim of religious/caste causal primacy.
5. The Church–kinship argument becomes less secure as it moves from rules to kinship change and then to impersonal political institutions.
6. Functional comparison, convergence language, and success-case selection can retrospectively organize plural histories around a privileged endpoint.
7. Institutional function does not settle justice, legitimacy, rights, equality, distribution, representation, obligation, or legitimate coercion.

## Fukuyamian Responses

- Comparative theory necessarily abstracts, and ideal types need not perfectly describe mixed cases.
- A broad accountability concept captures historically important non-electoral responsiveness and constraint.
- A restricted administrative definition of modernity permits comparison across conventional period boundaries.
- Macrohistory legitimately synthesizes mechanisms across specialized literatures and cannot demand experimental causal isolation.
- Recurrent coordination problems may produce functionally similar solutions without historical inevitability.
- Evolutionary variation, selection, adaptation, agency, borrowing, failure, and reversibility are compatible with contingency.
- A theory of political development need not be a complete normative political philosophy.

## Researcher Arguments

- A diagnostic framework can be philosophically valuable without being exhaustive or universally sequential.
- Causal confidence should decrease as the distance and number of intervening mechanisms in a macrohistorical chain increase.
- Universal political problems do not entail universal institutional functions at every level, and comparable functions do not entail universal forms or sequences.
- Abstraction becomes objectionable when it removes a difference needed to explain the outcome.
- Treating the triad as comparative questions—can authority act, is it bound, and to whom does it respond—preserves its value while reducing convergence-oriented pressure.
- Institutional origin, function, and normative justification remain distinct.
