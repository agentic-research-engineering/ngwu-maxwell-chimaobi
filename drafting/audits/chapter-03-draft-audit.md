## Contested Claims Used

No registered contested historical causal claim is required. Three interpretive disputes are presented with controls:

1. whether Fukuyama's natural-sociality argument defeats Hobbesian atomism;
2. whether Locke's state of nature is meaningfully presocial;
3. whether Rousseau's conjectural natural human can be tested as empirical anthropology.

Each is presented as position, qualification, and assessment. The chapter concludes that empirical anthropology can correct assumptions without automatically invalidating structural, juridical, or conjectural functions.

## Qualifications Preserved

- Fukuyama is not described as strictly deterministic.
- Human cooperation is explained through plural biological and cultural mechanisms.
- Bands and tribes are not treated as necessary universal stages.
- Hobbes's war is not continuous combat or simple psychological egoism.
- Locke's state of nature is not identical with war or total isolation.
- “Noble savage” is treated as inherited shorthand, not Rousseau's precise doctrine.
- Anthropology does not automatically refute philosophical state-of-nature arguments.
- Genealogy does not by itself establish legitimacy or justice.
