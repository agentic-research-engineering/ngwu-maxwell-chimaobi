# Thesis Style Sheet

## Scope and governing principle

This sheet governs the five substantive chapter drafts during integration. It records current practice; it does not authorize final submission formatting. The department's eventual style guide supersedes any conflicting convention below.

## Language and mechanics

- Use British English with Oxford `-ize` forms: *behaviour*, *defence*, *labour*, but *organization*, *centralize*, and *recognize*.
- Preserve spelling in titles and direct quotations.
- Use the serial comma where it prevents ambiguity.
- Prefer restrained academic prose. Avoid rhetorical intensifiers, unsupported claims of novelty, and claims that a source “proves” a contested causal proposition.
- Write historical periods and institutional names consistently; capitalize only proper names.

## Names, titles, and key terms

- First full reference: Francis Fukuyama, *The Origins of Political Order: From Prehuman Times to the French Revolution*; thereafter *Origins* in notes and *The Origins of Political Order* in prose where clarity requires it.
- First full reference: *Political Order and Political Decay: From the Industrial Revolution to the Globalization of Democracy*; thereafter *Political Order and Political Decay*.
- Italicize book titles and journal titles. Put article and chapter titles in quotation marks.
- Use **state**, **state capacity**, **state scope**, **government**, **regime**, **rule of law**, **rule by law**, **political accountability**, **democracy**, **political order**, **political development**, **political decay**, **institution**, **impersonality**, and **kinship** according to the distinctions fixed in Chapter One and tracked in the concept-consistency matrix.
- Do not use *modern state* without stating the relevant dimension. In the China discussion it means administrative centralization and bureaucratic organization, not liberal democracy or modern society as a whole.
- Do not equate accountability with democracy. Specify responsiveness, answerability, sanction, representation, consultation, or electoral accountability as appropriate.
- Do not treat kinship as either an undifferentiated obstacle or a primitive residue. State the institutional mechanism at issue.

## Attribution and epistemic force

- Attribute an authorial proposition explicitly with formulations such as “Fukuyama argues,” “on Fukuyama's account,” or “this study interprets.”
- Reserve *shows* and *establishes* for evidence that warrants that strength. Prefer *supports*, *suggests*, *is consistent with*, *helps explain*, or *is plausible* for qualified causal claims.
- Mark the researcher's synthesis through phrases such as “this study argues,” “the reconstructed framework,” or “the present evaluation.”
- Preserve disagreement rather than converting a contested proposition into an unqualified fact.
- Distinguish historical description, Fukuyama's interpretation, specialist assessment, and the researcher's philosophical judgment.

## Quotations and citations

- Use direct quotation only where exact wording matters. Verify every quotation against the named edition before submission.
- Current “drafting PDF” and “canonical ebook PDF” page references are research locators, not normalized submission citations.
- Do not infer print pagination from PDF offsets. Retain chapter, section, paragraph, or section-number locators where stable; normalize Fukuyama pagination only through an edition concordance or the final cited edition.
- Use footnotes for source support and compact qualifications, not for parallel arguments that belong in the body.
- Every cited source must appear in the final bibliography, and every final bibliography entry must either be cited or deliberately retained under an explicit departmental rule.
- Repository notes, dossiers, ledgers, abstracts, and search snippets are not authorities in the submitted thesis.

## Chapter architecture and transitions

- Chapter One frames the problem, questions, objectives, method, scope, and definitions.
- Chapter Two maps the literature and establishes the interpretive dispute.
- Chapter Three reconstructs the political anthropology and state-of-nature comparison.
- Chapter Four reconstructs institutions, mechanisms, interaction, and decay.
- Chapter Five evaluates the framework, answers objections, states the verdict, and concludes.
- Use brief backward references only when they perform a new argumentative task. Do not re-summarize full earlier sections.

## Preferred analytical distinctions

- common political problem / different historical sequence
- functional comparison / universal developmental path
- diagnostic framework / deterministic law
- administrative impersonality / absence of social relations
- legal autonomy / liberal constitutionalism
- social constraint / democratic accountability
- institutional development / moral progress
- explanation / evaluation

## Prohibited overstatements

- Do not call the theory strictly deterministic.
- Do not call the triad a sufficient formula for justice, legitimacy, or democracy.
- Do not claim that war alone produced the Chinese state.
- Do not claim that religion alone caused Indian state weakness.
- Do not describe military slavery as fully impersonal administration.
- Do not describe the Papal Revolution as an instantaneous origin of autonomous law.
- Do not claim that Church marriage rules by themselves caused Western individualism or modern political institutions.
- Do not claim that institutional balance guarantees stability.

## Status

The integrated drafts substantially conform to this sheet. Final Word styles, margins, heading numbering, note formatting, title-page conventions, and bibliography typography remain submission-stage work.
