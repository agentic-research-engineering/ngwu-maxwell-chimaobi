# Abstract Revision Plan

## Purpose

This is a revision plan, not a final abstract. The final abstract should be written only after substantive freeze and citation normalization.

## Elements that remain accurate

- The principal object is Fukuyama's account of political order, centred on *The Origins of Political Order* and continued by *Political Order and Political Decay*.
- The thesis examines the state, rule of law, and political accountability.
- It connects Fukuyama's institutional theory to his account of human sociality and compares that account with classical state-of-nature arguments.
- It uses the comparative histories of China, India, Islamic political orders, and Western Europe to test the theory.
- It evaluates the tension between comparative explanatory power and historical diversity or contingency.

## Elements that are superseded or require narrowing

- Replace any claim that the three institutions automatically produce stable order with the narrower claim that their differentiated interaction supplies a diagnostic framework for capacity, restraint, responsiveness, and decay.
- Avoid describing the theory as a comprehensive or universal explanation. The final verdict rejects strict determinism and treats recurrent functions as compatible with plural sequences.
- Remove unsupported prestige formulations such as “one of the most influential” unless they serve a necessary, sourced purpose.
- Do not imply that Fukuyama himself explicitly labels his theory a “diagnostic framework.” That term names the researcher's defended reconstruction.
- Replace a generic reference to “cultural diversity” with the precise problem of abstraction: whether omitted historical differences alter institutional classification, causal mechanism, direction, or normative implication.

## Results the final abstract must add

- Method: philosophical reconstruction and critical comparison, supported by primary texts and relevant specialist scholarship; no new empirical fieldwork.
- Main interpretive result: Fukuyama's account is not best read as a single necessary sequence, but neither is it free from convergence-oriented pressure.
- Main analytical result: the triad is valuable because it separates capacity, legal restraint, and accountability and permits analysis of imbalance and decay.
- Main qualification: the framework becomes distorting when functional abstraction suppresses differences that change the institutional or causal judgment.
- Main verdict: the theory is defensible as a qualified diagnostic framework, not as a deterministic or universally sufficient model of political development.
- Scope limit: the thesis does not conduct a contemporary country case study or establish a quantitative causal model.

## Suggested abstract sequence

1. One sentence stating the philosophical problem and principal text.
2. One sentence stating the research question and objects of analysis.
3. One sentence stating the method and comparative materials.
4. Two sentences stating the reconstruction: political anthropology, the triad, differentiated origins, interaction, and decay.
5. Two sentences stating the evaluation, strongest qualification, and defended interpretation.
6. One concise conclusion stating the contribution and limits.

## Control limits

- Target the department's required word count once confirmed; do not pad.
- Introduce no source, example, claim, or conclusion absent from the frozen thesis.
- Use no citations unless departmental rules require them in abstracts.
- Do not write the final abstract until the governing thesis and final conclusion have been frozen.
