# Chapter Two Literature Review Plan

## Planning Parameters

- **Authorized structure:** `# CHAPTER TWO` followed directly by `## 2.1 Literature Review`; the authoritative TOC supplies no separate Chapter Two title.
- **Method:** thematic scholarly synthesis, not author-by-author summary.
- **Provisional body range after mapping:** 3,200–4,500 words. This reflects seven linked conversations and remains shorter than the combined substantive chapters.
- **Research-gap standard:** modest integrative gap; no comprehensive novelty claim.
- **Research status:** existing Phase 2 evidence is sufficient. No new research is required before drafting.

## Source-Balance Audit Before Drafting

- **Direct scholarly engagement with Fukuyama used substantively:** 5 secondary works (Hewson, Manning, Montanye, Mungiu-Pippidi, Melleuish within abstract limits), plus Fukuyama's methodological self-clarification as a primary source.
- **Conceptual political/institutional scholarship used substantively:** Bovens; Huntington only as bounded intellectual background because full text is absent.
- **Specialist historical/anthropological scholarship represented:** China, India, Islamic military households, canon law, Church/kinship, and evolutionary anthropology; more than a dozen adopted works, synthesized rather than individually summarized.
- **Primary philosophical texts:** none used as secondary scholarship; Hobbes, Locke, and Rousseau remain Chapter Three primary objects.
- **Institutional reports:** none.
- **Tertiary sources:** none.
- **Assessment:** sufficient scholarly coverage for a bounded undergraduate literature review. The direct-Fukuyama corpus is too limited for claims of disciplinary consensus, so independent scholarship is used as theory-testing literature and the gap statement remains integrative.

## Block Plan

- **Block A:** Fukuyama's project, macro-theory, institutional development, the state, capacity, bureaucracy, impersonality, patrimonialism, and decay.
- **Block B:** rule of law, institutional autonomy, accountability, and representative historical/comparative disputes.
- **Block C:** evolutionary explanation, universalism, convergence, alternative paths, synthesis of unresolved questions, and modest research gap.
