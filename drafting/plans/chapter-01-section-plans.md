# Chapter One Section Plans

## Planning Parameters

- **Authorized chapter:** Chapter One only, using the exact approved headings.
- **Provisional body range:** 3,500–4,500 words. The introduction must establish the problem, method, scope, and concepts but remain smaller than the central reconstruction and evaluation chapters.
- **Primary textual basis:** Fukuyama's *The Origins of Political Order* (2011).
- **Companion use:** *Political Order and Political Decay* (2014) for later development, capacity, decay, adaptation, repatrimonialization, and convergence-related clarification.
- **Non-prejudgment control:** Chapter One frames the inquiry; it does not present Chapter Five's complete verdict as an initial premise.
- **Research status:** No new research required.

## 1.1 Background to the Study

- **SECTION QUESTION:** Why does Fukuyama's institutional theory present a significant philosophical problem?
- **SECTION FUNCTION:** Narrow from the perennial problem of organized power to Fukuyama's triad and the need for investigation.
- **REQUIRED CONTENT:** Need for effective authority, constraint, responsiveness; historical difficulty of institutional development; Fukuyama's comparative ambition; triad.
- **CLAIMS:** Political order is multidimensional; capacity, law, and accountability are distinct; institutions develop historically and may decay.
- **SOURCES:** *Origins* ch. 1 and chs. 29–30; *Political Order and Political Decay* chs. 1–2 and 31–36.
- **CONCEPTS:** Political order, state capacity, rule of law, accountability, development, decay.
- **RELATION TO RESEARCH QUESTION:** Establishes the object and why explanatory adequacy matters.
- **RELATION TO LATER CHAPTERS:** Leads to Chapter Two's debates and Chapters Three–Five's reconstruction/evaluation.
- **WHAT MUST NOT APPEAR:** Generic history, Nigeria, detailed civilizational cases, final evaluative verdict.
- **CITATION NEEDS:** Primary citations for Fukuyama's architecture and historical-development claims.
- **REPETITION RISKS:** Duplicating Chapters Two and Three; keep background synthetic.
- **UNRESOLVED ISSUES:** None blocking.

## 1.2 Statement of the Problem

- **SECTION QUESTION:** What precise philosophical tension does the study investigate?
- **SECTION FUNCTION:** Formulate reconstructive and evaluative tasks and converge on the central research question.
- **REQUIRED CONTENT:** Distinctness and interaction of triadic institutions; cross-historical comparison; category breadth; causal chains; universal problems versus plural mechanisms; convergence without strict determinism.
- **CLAIMS:** Macrocomparison creates a tension between analytical abstraction and historical plurality.
- **SOURCES:** Completed scholarly conversation and verified primary architecture.
- **CONCEPTS:** Functional categories, ideal types, historical contingency, convergence, causal explanation.
- **RELATION TO RESEARCH QUESTION:** States the central question explicitly.
- **RELATION TO LATER CHAPTERS:** Maps directly onto Chapters Three–Five.
- **WHAT MUST NOT APPEAR:** Final thesis answer, strict-determinism allegation, necessary universal sequence claim.
- **CITATION NEEDS:** Minimal; primary triad citation and literature-review basis where needed.
- **REPETITION RISKS:** Reproducing Chapter Five's criticisms and conclusion.
- **UNRESOLVED ISSUES:** Questions must remain genuinely open.
