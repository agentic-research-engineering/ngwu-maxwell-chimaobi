## Major Analytical Results

- **Accountability:** Fukuyama uses a graded concept encompassing moral, institutional, and electoral forms. This supports the claim that accountability is broader than democracy, but exposes a threshold problem: inward responsibility without an external sanction may not count as accountability in a stricter sense.
- **Determinism:** Strict historical determinism is not supported. Fukuyama expressly denies that inherited paths fix future outcomes and uses variation, selection, contingency, and institutional interaction. The stronger live objections are functionalism, retrospective case selection, and the use of a universal institutional endpoint.
- **State of nature:** Hobbes, Locke, Rousseau, and Fukuyama are not offering four equivalent empirical prehistories. Hobbes supplies a structural authority problem; Locke a juridical condition under natural law; Rousseau a conjectural genealogy of dependence; Fukuyama an evolutionary-anthropological reconstruction. The comparison must preserve these different functions.
- **China:** Early centralized territorial bureaucracy is well supported, but “first modern state” is definition-dependent. It is defensible under Fukuyama's restricted administrative criterion and misleading under a fuller constitutional conception of modernity. Warfare was important, but its causal sufficiency and the date of mature bureaucracy remain contested.

## Specialist Historical Verification
