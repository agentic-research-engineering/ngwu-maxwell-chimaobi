**Resolution:** P0-A is **RESOLVED WITH A LITERATURE-BASE QUALIFICATION**. Direct reception remains numerically review-heavy, but the project no longer relies on reviews alone: Mungiu-Pippidi supplies a substantive theory-level test, Fukuyama's peer-reviewed methodological clarification fixes the author's position, and independent institutional/state-capacity scholarship tests the central concepts. This is enough for a bounded undergraduate literature review, not for a claim of settled disciplinary consensus.

## P0-B — India claim decomposition

| Claim | Specialist evidence | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| IND-1 Strong kin/social structures persisted | Dharmaśāstra and caste scholarship confirms durable, locally embedded status and customary orders, but `kinship` must not be treated as synonymous with caste. | **SUPPORTED WITH QUALIFICATION** |
| IND-2 Varṇa/jāti organized society beyond a unitary central state | Patrick Olivelle's legal-history synthesis and the *Oxford History of Hinduism* distinguish normative varṇa from varied social practice; both show the wide social reach of Brahmanical categories. | **SUPPORTED**, with the ideal/practice distinction mandatory. |
| IND-3 Brahmanical authority was partly outside direct royal command | Timothy Lubin's study of religious endowments shows tax, labor, property, and jurisdictional immunities that institutionalized Brahmin authority; Furui shows Brahmin networks extending through court and rural society. | **SUPPORTED**, especially for the first millennium CE; not timeless. |
| IND-4 Religious/social authority constrained rulers | Dharma theory subordinated royal law to dharma and assigned interpretive competence to learned Brahmins. Yet rulers also patronized, juridified, and materially produced Brahmin privilege. | **SUPPORTED WITH IMPORTANT QUALIFICATION**: reciprocal constitution, not a simple Church-versus-state check. |
| IND-5 Mauryan government achieved substantial centralization | R. S. Sharma links mature states to taxation and professional armies; Gérard Fussman confirms an extensive Mauryan polity. | **SUPPORTED** |
| IND-6 Its reach and continuity differed from imperial China | Fussman finds flexible, uneven control strongest near the core. The comparison is plausible, but institutional durability cannot be reduced to a single China/India scale. | **SUPPORTED WITH QUALIFICATION** |
| IND-7 Autonomous society caused weaker centralization | Evidence establishes coexistence and reciprocal interaction, not a clean causal direction. State grants helped constitute autonomous Brahmin institutions; regional ecology, communications, war, agrarian organization, and political strategy are rival causes. | **PLAUSIBLE BUT UNDERDETERMINED** |
| IND-8 Religion was the major divergence mechanism | Mark McClish dates a strong Brahmanical influence on statecraft to the early centuries CE, warning against projecting it backward onto an already distinct statecraft tradition. | **CONTESTED / INSUFFICIENT FOR PRIMACY** |

**Resolution:** Fukuyama's descriptive contrast survives only in bounded form: India combined real large-scale states with durable, partly autonomous social and normative orders and uneven territorial penetration. The stronger arrow `religion/caste → weak centralization` is **PLAUSIBLE BUT UNDERDETERMINED**, not verified. “Brahmanical authority” must not be redescribed as political accountability in Fukuyama's procedural sense.

## P0-C — Western Christianity, family rules, and kinship

| Claim | Evidence and counterevidence | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| CHR-1 distinctive Western Church family regime | Canon-law and family-history scholarship, including Jack Goody's synthesis, establishes distinctive ecclesiastical regulation of marriage. | **VERIFIED** |
| CHR-2 content of the rules | Consanguinity/affinity impediments, monogamy, indissolubility, consent, and limits on remarriage are well documented. Adoption, inheritance, polygyny, and clerical celibacy belong to different periods and mechanisms and must not be bundled into one decree. | **VERIFIED WITH PERIODIZATION** |
| CHR-3 weakened extended kinship | Goody argues for the causal effect; Schulz et al., *Science* 366 (2019), find a historical-exposure association between the Western Church's marriage program, less intensive kinship, and psychological variation. | **SUPPORTED WITH IMPORTANT QUALIFICATION**: enforcement was uneven and the modern study is observational rather than a medieval natural experiment. |
| CHR-4 nuclear/individual property arrangements | Goody and later comparative work support a directional link, but medieval household forms and bilateral kin ties remained varied. | **SUPPORTED WITH QUALIFICATION** |
| CHR-5 inheritance/property effects | Church rules and corporate property interests affected inheritance opportunities; motives cannot be reduced to institutional land hunger. | **PLAUSIBLE AND DEBATED** |
| CHR-6 weakened kinship caused impersonal institutions | Cross-regional correlations and newer institutional work make the mechanism plausible. Historians warn that medieval kinship persisted and was recomposed rather than simply destroyed. | **CONTESTED BUT RESEARCHED** |
| CHR-7 uniquely differentiated the West | Western/Orthodox comparison supports institutional variation, but “Western exceptionalism” risks selecting the outcome and compressing regional diversity. | **CONTESTED** |

**Resolution:** P0-C is **RESOLVED BY QUALIFICATION**. The rules and their association with less intensive kinship are supported; Fukuyama's stronger linear sequence to individualism and impersonal political order is not established as a sole or sufficient cause. The project will say “weakened or reorganized some intensive kin structures under uneven enforcement,” not “destroyed kinship.”

## P0-D — Evolutionary-anthropology claim map

| Claim | Current specialist assessment | Verdict on Fukuyama's use |
|---|---|---|
| EVO-1 humans are naturally social | Reviews characterize humans as ultrasocial; primate, developmental, and forager evidence decisively rejects an originally isolated empirical human. | **WELL SUPPORTED** |
| EVO-2 kin selection contributes | Remains a canonical mechanism. | **SUPPORTED, NOT SUFFICIENT** |
| EVO-3 reciprocity contributes beyond kin | Direct and indirect reciprocity, reputation, partner choice, punishment, and norms all matter. | **SUPPORTED, BUT “RECIPROCAL ALTRUISM” ALONE IS TOO NARROW** |
| EVO-4 status/recognition has deep roots | Comparative primate and human status research makes evolved status motivation plausible. Mapping this directly onto Hegelian recognition is philosophical synthesis, not biological verification. | **PLAUSIBLE INTERPRETATION** |
| EVO-5 early organization was band-level, not isolated | Hill et al. (2011) find flexible forager co-residence with few close biological kin and many affines/nonkin. Bands are real residential forms, but not closed kin units. | **SUPPORTED WITH CORRECTION** |
| EVO-6 tribes were later formations, not humanity's original universal form | Political anthropology treats “tribe” as historically loaded and rejects a mandatory band→tribe→chiefdom→state ladder. Comparative evolutionary methods can retain types only as revisable heuristics. | **FUKUYAMA'S NON-ORIGINAL-TRIBE POINT IS PLAUSIBLE; STAGE VOCABULARY IS OUTDATED IF LINEARIZED** |
| EVO-7 religion enabled larger cooperation | Purzycki et al. (2016) support an association between moralizing punitive gods and broader co-religionist cooperation; reviews treat religion as one mechanism among several. | **SUPPORTED WITH SCOPE LIMITS** |
| EVO-8 warfare promoted political scaling | Warfare affected cities, states, and empires, but evolutionary anthropology treats its origins and causal weight as hotly debated. | **PLAUSIBLE BUT CONTESTED; NOT NECESSARY OR SUFFICIENT** |
| EVO-9 kin favoritism creates patrimonial pressure | Kin bias is real, but flexible nonkin networks and culturally built institutions complicate a direct biological-to-patrimonial inference. | **PLAUSIBLE RECURRING PRESSURE, NOT DESTINY** |
| EVO-10 impersonal institutions must channel these tendencies | A defensible institutional inference, not a direct finding of evolutionary science. | **RESEARCHER/FUKUYAMA INFERENCE** |

Key controls include Henrich and Muthukrishna's 2021 review of multiple cooperation mechanisms; Lee's 2018 review emphasizing cooperative breeding, conflict management, flexible tenure, and gender balance; Hill et al.'s 32-society co-residence study; Handley and Mathew's 2020 evidence for culturally structured large-scale cooperation; and specialist reviews treating war as genuinely disputed.

**State-of-nature implication:** Current anthropology supports Fukuyama against an *empirical* picture of naturally isolated presocial individuals. It does **not** invalidate Hobbes's structural thought experiment, Locke's juridical condition, or Rousseau's explicitly conjectural genealogy. The empirical and philosophical questions remain separate.

**Resolution:** P0-D is **RESOLVED WITH REVISION**. The sociality premise survives; the causal architecture must be plural-mechanism and gene–culture coevolutionary; “band/tribe” must be heuristic, not a universal ladder; religion and war are qualified mechanisms rather than master causes.
