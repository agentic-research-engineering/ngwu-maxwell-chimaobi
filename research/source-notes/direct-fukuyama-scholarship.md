## Martin Hewson

## Conversation synthesis

The usable direct literature supports three conversations rather than a single verdict: (1) admiration for the project's comparative scope and differentiation of institutions; (2) criticism of universal evolutionary classification, case selection, and historical compression; and (3) questions about convergence and whether the triad captures the full range of political innovation.

## P0-A supplement

### Alina Mungiu-Pippidi

- **WORK:** “The Evolution of Political Order,” *Journal of Democracy* 26, no. 4 (2015): 169–175.
- **DISCIPLINE / STATUS:** Comparative politics; scholarly journal essay directly engaging both volumes.
- **ACCESS:** Full paginated HTML accessed.
- **ARGUMENT:** Fukuyama's cases display multiple paths, but his evolutionary/convergence theory lacks a persuasive classification scale and adequate evidence. She questions the primacy of state capacity and the movement between institutions as rules and the state as an organization.
- **METHOD / USE:** Conceptual and comparative evaluation; direct support for a bounded convergence, classification, and functional-compression critique.
- **LIMITATION:** Compact essay rather than an independent historical case test.

### Methodological self-clarification and comparators

Fukuyama's peer-reviewed “Macro Theory and the Study of Political Development” (2016) fixes the author's methodological position but is primary self-interpretation, not independent validation. Recent historical-political-economy scholarship disaggregating coercive, extractive, and legal capacities tests his architecture even when it does not name him; it is theory-testing scholarship, not direct reception.
