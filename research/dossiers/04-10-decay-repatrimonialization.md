## Closure Finding

`[VOLUME II]` Chapter 31 defines decay at the level of particular institutions and identifies nonadaptation/cognitive rigidity and insider capture/repatrimonialization as recurring mechanisms (canonical PDF pp. 483–488). Chapter 8 extends repatrimonialization to legal interest-group capture while conceding that patrimonial pressure is recurrent rather than terminal (pp. 221–223). The dossier can support exposition; contemporary applications require separate current evidence.

## Phase 2 Findings

`[FUKUYAMA]` Decay occurs when sticky institutions fail to adapt to changed circumstances (ch. 1, PDF p. 21; ch. 29, p. 472). Repatrimonialization is the re-entry of kin/personal control into impersonal institutions (chs. 5 and 15, PDF pp. 96, 241–247). `[INTERPRETATION]` Fukuyama offers two connected mechanisms: environmental maladaptation and capture by entrenched groups. Volume II evidence is still required before this dossier becomes research ready.
