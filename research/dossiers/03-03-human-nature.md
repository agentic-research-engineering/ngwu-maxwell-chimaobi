## Phase 2 Findings

`[FUKUYAMA]` Fukuyama identifies kin selection and reciprocal altruism as evolved sources of cooperation (ch. 2, PDF pp. 44–45), adds norm-following and recognition/status, and treats these dispositions as probabilistic foundations rather than complete institutional causes. `[SCHOLAR]` Hewson reads the biological account as non-determinist and credits its integration of nepotism, religion, norms, and recognition. `[CONTESTED]` The transition from primate/evolutionary evidence to political explanation and the relation between Hegelian recognition and evolved status require the qualified treatment recorded below.

## P0 Closure Finding

Specialist evidence supports natural sociality and roles for kin selection and reciprocity, but contemporary accounts require reputation, punishment, norms, cooperative breeding, culture, and flexible nonkin networks. Status has plausible evolutionary roots; equating it with Hegelian recognition remains an interpretation. The section can be drafted if this plural-mechanism correction is maintained.
