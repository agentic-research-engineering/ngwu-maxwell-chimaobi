## Phase 2 Findings

The comparison now has primary-text anchors: Hobbes uses an authority-free condition to expose structural insecurity; Locke describes moral relations under natural law without a common judge; Rousseau offers an explicitly conjectural abstraction tracing dependence and inequality; Fukuyama proposes an evolutionary/anthropological reconstruction. `[INTERPRETATION]` These are not four interchangeable empirical hypotheses. The central philosophical task is to compare their explanatory functions while preserving genre, normativity, and evidential method.

## Closure Adversarial Finding

The claim that Fukuyama simply mistakes thought experiments for empirical histories is too strong. His evidence legitimately tests anthropological assumptions embedded in the models, but it cannot displace their structural, juridical, conjectural, and normative functions. The criticism is strongest against his simplified treatment of Rousseau and weakest when directed at Hobbes's authority problem. Editions are resolved; cite Hobbes by chapter, Locke by section, and Rousseau from the Cress/Hackett 1992 edition after page-level consultation.
