## Phase 2 Primary Findings

`[LOCKE-PRIMARY]` *Second Treatise* §§4–15 presents a state of freedom and equality governed by natural law and reason, with individuals holding executive power to punish violations. It is not identical to the state of war (§§16–21). Property can arise before government through labour under moral limits (ch. V). Consent forms political society (§95), and government remedies insecure enjoyment of life, liberty, and estate (§123). `[FUKUYAMA-ON-LOCKE]` Fukuyama's “presocial individual” contrast requires qualification: Locke's analysis includes family, natural law, cooperation, property, and moral obligation prior to political society. SEP confirms that the distinction between state of nature and state of war is central but scholarly interpretation of the relational state of nature is contested. Final citations must use the Macpherson/Hackett edition named in the bibliography or explicitly adopt another edition.
