## Competing Interpretations

Melleuish's historical critique targets a universal evolutionary model rather than simple inevitability. A stronger internal objection asks whether cases are retrospectively selected and functionally classified by their movement toward a universal triad.

## Philosophical Analysis

Strict determinism entails inevitability from antecedent conditions; the text rejects this. Path dependence, functional explanation, teleology, and universalism are distinct propositions and must be tested separately. The live criticism concerns the explanatory architecture, not a claim that every society follows one necessary sequence.

## Phase 2 Findings

`[FUKUYAMA]` Fukuyama explicitly says past paths do not determine future possibilities (ch. 1, PDF pp. 36–37) and anticipates a determinism objection in ch. 29 (PDF p. 457). `[SCHOLAR]` Hewson characterizes the biological element as probabilistic, not deterministic. `[SCHOLAR/CONTESTED]` Melleuish argues a universal evolutionary model is historically insufficient. `[RESEARCHER-THESIS]` A defensible critique should distinguish strict determinism (poorly supported) from teleological selection, functionalism, retrospective classification, or universal evolutionary modeling. Full secondary articles and case evidence remain P0.

## Closure Adversarial Finding

Strict determinism is **REJECTED**. `[VOLUME II]` Chapter 36 makes agency, iterative learning, divergence, and convergence explicit (PDF pp. 549–560). The defensible replacement critique separates functionalism, retrospective selection, universal evolutionary modeling, convergence, and teleological residue. Fukuyama himself admits deliberate success-case selection for the war/state-building comparison (ch. 8, PDF pp. 222–223). The remaining argument is bounded and can be drafted without representing it as scholarly consensus.
