# Citation Style Audit

## Source of Style Authority

The working precedent is `FRANCIS FUKUYAMA_075051.docx`, a November 2025 PHI 305 group project from Bigard Memorial Seminary, Enugu, in affiliation with the University of Ibadan. It is classified **DEPARTMENTAL STYLE / PRECEDENT DOCUMENT** and, at most, a source-discovery aid. It has no substantive authority over this project's verified research.

The sample's citation content is inconsistent and sometimes incomplete. The governing rule is therefore:

> SAMPLE FORMAT + VERIFIED RESEARCH METADATA = PROJECT CITATION.

## Citation System

The document uses true Word **footnotes** with superscript Arabic callouts. Notes appear at the foot of the page beneath a separator rule and in smaller type. Parenthetical author–date citation is not the demonstrated system. A bibliography follows the body.

## Demonstrated rules

### First citation — book

**SAMPLE-DERIVED:** Given name Surname, *Full Title* (Place: Publisher, Year), p. X.

Example structure: `Francis Fukuyama, *The Origins of Political Order* (New York: Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 2011), p. 7.`

The sample also contains bare page numbers. For project consistency, use `p.` for one page and `pp.` for a range unless a stable section/chapter locator is required.

### Consecutive citation

**SAMPLE-DERIVED:** `Ibid., p. X.` / `Ibid., pp. X–Y.` The sample also demonstrates `Ibid.` where the same work and location repeat. Use `Ibid.` only when the immediately preceding note cites the same work unambiguously.

### Non-consecutive citation

**SAMPLE-DERIVED:** `Surname, *Short Title*, p. X.` The sample alternates between `Fukuyama, *Origins*, p. 103` and a fuller short title. Use one intelligible short title consistently.

### Edited and translated philosophical works

**SAMPLE-DERIVED:** editor/translator follows the italicized title before publication facts:

- `Thomas Hobbes, *Leviathan*, ed. Richard Tuck (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1996 [1651]), p. 89.`
- `Jean-Jacques Rousseau, *Discourse ...*, trans. [verified translator] (Place: Publisher, Year [original year]), pp. X–Y.`

Original publication year may appear in square brackets after the consulted edition year. Sections may precede pages, as in `§§4–6, p. 271`; use the stable section locator when philosophically material.

### Journal article

The Hamilton note demonstrates the broad order—author, quoted article title, journal title, volume, year, pages—but is typographically corrupt and omits issue information. A complete departmental form is **NOT ESTABLISHED BY SAMPLE**.

**PROJECT WORKING CONVENTION — REQUIRES CONFIRMATION IF DEPARTMENT GUIDANCE BECOMES AVAILABLE:** Given name Surname, “Article Title,” *Journal Title* volume, no. issue (Year): cited page(s), DOI/URL only when required for identification.

### Chapter in an edited volume

The Dong Zhongshu citation demonstrates `Work Title, in Collection Title, ed. Editor (Place: Publisher, Year), p. X`, but not a modern authored chapter with inclusive pages.

**PROJECT EXTENSION:** Given name Surname, “Chapter Title,” in *Book Title*, ed. Editor Name (Place: Publisher, Year), cited page; bibliography adds full chapter range.

### Institutional report

**SAMPLE-DERIVED AT BASIC LEVEL:** Corporate Author, *Report Title* (Place: Publisher, Year), p. X. Bibliography: Corporate Author. *Report Title*. Place: Publisher, Year.

The sample contains World Bank and IMF instances but is inconsistent about place and series/report number. Use verified issuing-body metadata.

### Web source

The bibliography includes institutional web publications but does not demonstrate URLs, access dates, or a reliable web-footnote pattern. **NOT ESTABLISHED BY SAMPLE.**

**PROJECT EXTENSION:** Author/Institution, “Page or Report Title,” site/publisher, publication or revision date, URL (accessed Day Month Year). Omit an access date where a stable DOI and publication date make it unnecessary, subject to supervisor direction.

## Bibliography

The sample uses a terminal bibliography, alphabetized by author or corporate author. Personal names are inverted (`Surname, Given name`). Book titles are italicized. Entries use periods between major elements and the order `Author. *Title*. Place: Publisher, Year.` Translated/edited works spell out `Translated by` or `Edited by`. Original years can appear in square brackets. The rendered sample shows numbered entries and hanging-style alignment; no primary/secondary division is consistently demonstrated, and repeated authors are written again rather than replaced by a long dash.

### Book

**SAMPLE-DERIVED:** `Surname, Given name. *Full Title*. Place: Publisher, Year.`

### Primary philosophical text

**SAMPLE-DERIVED:** `Surname, Given name. *Title*. Edited/Translated by Name. Place: Publisher, consulted-edition year [original year].`

### Journal

**PROJECT EXTENSION:** `Surname, Given name. “Article Title.” *Journal Title* volume, no. issue (Year): full page range. DOI.`

## Page numbers and locators

The sample inconsistently uses `p.`, `pp.`, bare numbers, ranges, and section signs. The normalized project convention is:

- `p. 7` for one print/PDF page;
- `pp. 7–9` for a range, using an en dash;
- `§4` or `§§4–6` for stable numbered sections;
- chapter plus canonical PDF page where the repository's pagination audit shows that print pagination cannot yet be mapped.

Never manufacture print pages. Stable chapter/section/PDF locators remain acceptable working research locators until final edition mapping.

## Capitalization, italics, and quotations

The sample uses headline-style capitalization for English titles, italics for standalone books/reports, and quotation marks for articles. Short quotations are integrated into paragraphs with double quotation marks and a superscript note after the closing punctuation. No sufficiently consistent block-quotation rule is established; treat long-quotation layout as **PROJECT EXTENSION** pending department guidance and use an indented block without quotation marks.

## Template library

### Book

- **FIRST FOOTNOTE — SAMPLE-DERIVED:** `Given Surname, *Title* (Place: Publisher, Year), p. X.`
- **SUBSEQUENT — SAMPLE-DERIVED:** `Surname, *Short Title*, p. X.`
- **BIBLIOGRAPHY — SAMPLE-DERIVED:** `Surname, Given. *Title*. Place: Publisher, Year.`

### Translated classical work

- **FIRST — SAMPLE-DERIVED:** `Given Surname, *Title*, trans. Translator (Place: Publisher, Year [original year]), p. X.`
- **SUBSEQUENT — SAMPLE-DERIVED:** `Surname, *Short Title*, p. X.`
- **BIBLIOGRAPHY — SAMPLE-DERIVED:** `Surname, Given. *Title*. Translated by Translator. Place: Publisher, Year [original year].`

### Edited work

- **FIRST — SAMPLE-DERIVED:** `Given Surname, *Title*, ed. Editor (Place: Publisher, Year [original year]), p. X.`
- **BIBLIOGRAPHY — SAMPLE-DERIVED:** `Surname, Given. *Title*. Edited by Editor. Place: Publisher, Year [original year].`

### Journal article

- **PROJECT EXTENSION:** `Given Surname, “Article Title,” *Journal* volume, no. issue (Year): p. X.`
- **BIBLIOGRAPHY:** `Surname, Given. “Article Title.” *Journal* volume, no. issue (Year): XX–YY. DOI.`

### Chapter in edited book

- **PROJECT EXTENSION:** `Given Surname, “Chapter,” in *Book*, ed. Editor (Place: Publisher, Year), p. X.`
- **BIBLIOGRAPHY:** `Surname, Given. “Chapter.” In *Book*, edited by Editor, XX–YY. Place: Publisher, Year.`

### Institutional report

- **SAMPLE-DERIVED BASIC FORM:** `Institution, *Report Title* (Place: Institution/Publisher, Year), p. X.`
- **BIBLIOGRAPHY:** `Institution. *Report Title*. Place: Institution/Publisher, Year.`

### Web source

- **PROJECT EXTENSION:** `Author/Institution, “Page Title,” Site, date, URL (accessed Day Month Year).`

## Thesis-formatting clues

The sample uses an institutional title page, numbered decimal headings, centered major headings, justified body paragraphs, and conventional footnotes. These are clues rather than a complete thesis-format specification; margins, font, line spacing, preliminary pages, and binding requirements remain subject to any later supervisor or departmental guide.

## Existing bibliography normalization policy

The verified metadata and defect classifications in `research/legacy-bibliography-audit.md` and `research-bible/16-bibliography-register.md` remain controlling. A source may be normalized only after author/editor/translator, full title, place, publisher/journal, year, volume/issue, pages, and DOI (where applicable) are verified. The class sample's incomplete Aristotle and Hegel entries, its questionable Rousseau edition data, and its malformed Hamilton article are not to be copied.

## P0-E decision

**RESOLVED.** The sample was inspected at OOXML, extracted-text, and rendered-page levels; the demonstrated system, its inconsistencies, and reusable templates are now documented. Unsupported source types are explicitly marked as project extensions.
